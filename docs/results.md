## Ethics and scope (SEC-011)

Testnet only, with valueless tokens. Every attack behind these numbers ran **against
this project's own services on a local chain** — never against a third-party endpoint,
never against a hosted facilitator, and never with real funds. The vulnerable fixture
exists solely as a measurement control and is labelled as such in its source, its logs,
its HTTP responses and every manifest it appears in.

The two attack classes reproduced here are already public: arXiv 2605.11781 §4.3 and
arXiv 2605.30998 §4.1. Nothing in this repository discloses a new vulnerability in
anyone else's software, and no result here is evidence about anyone else's
implementation.

## What the two targets are

**`agenttrust`** is the system as built: the escrow with its on-chain `resourceHash` and
reputation gate, the seller with its payer-signature check and atomic claim store, two
seller processes sharing one claim database.

**`fixture`** is a **deliberately vulnerable baseline written for this evaluation**
(`impl/attacks/src/vulnerable-server.ts`). It reproduces the conditions the two published
papers describe: an authorization verified off-chain, the resource granted immediately,
no idempotency and no binding between the payment and the request. It is **not** the
`@x402/*` packages, **not** upstream x402, and **not** evidence about anyone else's
implementation. Establishing anything about upstream would require running against a
pinned upstream version, which is API-009 and was **not done**.

## What held, and what it means

Both hypotheses held, and the fixture reproduced the published numbers closely enough to
be recognisable:

- **A2.** The fixture executed the work **once per request**: after a single payment,
  50 requests produced 50 executions and 200 produced 200, unchanged by concurrency.
  The published condition is 50 replays producing 50 grants with no idempotency (V-13).
  AgentTrust executed **once per payment** in every configuration — sequential, 50-way
  concurrent and 200-way concurrent, across two seller processes sharing one claim
  store. Across its six configurations, **4,440 replay attempts produced zero extra
  executions and zero unauthorised responses**.
- **A3.** The fixture served **100 of 100** substitutions — a payment for
  `/v1/summarise` buying `/v1/classify` — which matches the published F1 result of
  100/100 rounds (V-24). AgentTrust served **0 of 100**, refusing each with
  `resource_mismatch`.

The number that matters for A2 is **executions**, not HTTP 200s. Under
`REPLAY_POLICY=idempotent` a replay is *supposed* to return the stored bytes to the
authenticated payer; counting that as a failure would be as wrong as counting it as a
win. The counters are read from each system's own records — the claim store for
AgentTrust, the grant log for the fixture — never inferred from status codes.

### The control that makes the A3 zero meaningful

A server that refuses everything would also score 0/100. Each A3 round therefore also
checks that the **correct** request still succeeds, and that a **one-byte body change**
is refused. AgentTrust had **zero false refusals** and rejected **100 of 100** mutated
bodies; the fixture accepted all 100, because an EIP-3009 authorization names a payer, a
payee and an amount and says nothing whatever about which resource is being bought.

### What the forged-signature variant showed

The fixture grants on a **completely forged signature**: 500 of 500 requests served, all
of them unauthorised. It checks the amount, the payee and the validity window, and never
verifies the signature at all. That is not a replay finding — it means the baseline can
be robbed without replaying anything. AgentTrust served nothing to a forged signature, a
foreign one, or none at all.

The fixture does refuse a request with **no** authorization, which is worth stating
because it shows the zero in that row is a real check rather than an artefact of the
harness.

## What this does not show

- **Nothing about Base Sepolia.** Every run is on a local Anvil. Latency, gas and
  confirmation behaviour do not transfer, and no transaction has been sent to a public
  chain.
- **Nothing about upstream x402.** See above; API-009 was not run.
- **Four of the six defined attacks were not evaluated.** A1 (reorg), A4 (concurrent
  duplication), A5 (allowance overdraft) and A6 (Sybil selection) are EXTENDED-E2 and
  were not run. They are reported as "Not evaluated" rather than omitted, and the
  coverage figure carries its denominator.
- **"Blocked (structural)" is a claim about a mechanism, not a guarantee.** For A2 the
  mechanism is the claim store keyed by `(chainId, escrow, jobId)` together with the
  payer-signed retry; for A3 it is the `resourceHash` the escrow computes on-chain. Both
  are asserted by unit tests as well as measured here. Neither claim extends to an
  attacker who can do something these runs did not attempt.

## Known limitations that bear on these numbers

- The reputation gate reads `getSummary` per trusted client, and that read costs about
  **8,589 gas per feedback entry**; at the deployed 250,000-gas ceiling a client with
  more than roughly 26 entries is dropped from the gate
  (`evidence/CONTRACT-005/gas-vs-history.txt`).
- `requestHash` squatting is unbounded against an adversary watching the mempool, because
  the salt becomes public when the seller broadcasts (DF-06, corrected). The seller's
  retry budget is finite and folded into `minDeadlineMargin`.
- A validator that attests and then changes its mind before anyone snapshots the pass
  erases it (DF-05). The validator releases immediately to keep that window small.

## Latency, on a local devnet (EVAL-006)

Ten end-to-end runs of `impl/scripts/e2e-happy.sh`, each a complete paid job: quote,
gate, fund, signed retry, delivery, evidence deposit, validation binding, attestation
and release. Raw per-stage timestamps are in `evidence/EVAL-006/all-timings.ndjson`;
the table is produced by `impl/scripts/eval006-summary.py`.

| Stage | What happens | median | min | max | n |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `services_up` → `purchase_begin` | harness idle before the purchase | 8 ms | 7 ms | 11 ms | 10 |
| `purchase_begin` → `delivered` | quote, gate, fund, signed retry, delivery | 778 ms | 408 ms | 796 ms | 10 |
| `delivered` → `validator_decided` | evidence deposit, binding, attestation | 512 ms | 261 ms | 519 ms | 10 |
| `validator_decided` → `released` | release transaction | 4 ms | 3 ms | 9 ms | 10 |
| `purchase_begin` → `verified` | whole paid job, end to end | 1090 ms | 933 ms | 1336 ms | 10 |

`validator_decided` → `released` is small because it is not the release latency: the validator calls `release()` itself immediately after attesting (DF-15), so by the time the buyer polls, the transaction is already mined. That span measures the buyer noticing, not the chain settling — the release itself is inside `delivered` → `validator_decided`.

**These are Anvil numbers and must never be quoted as Base Sepolia ones.** Confirmations
are set to 0 and blocks are mined on demand, so the single largest cost of a real
deployment — waiting for blocks — is absent entirely. On Base Sepolia a 2-second block
time alone would dominate every row above. Whether the *shape* survives on a testnet is
INT-003, which is blocked on funding.
